- [x] Investigate 30 fps videos: most 30fpx videos work, but some stutter badly even though they play smoothly on PC. Example found 2026-10-10 (FC2-PPV-4808888, H.264 with B-frames): the file was muxed without composition offsets, so its pts equal its dts and are not in display order. FFmpeg's software decoder hides that by falling back to a dts-based timestamp; the Frame's V4L2 decoder returns the packet times as they are, so the pictures (which come out in display order) carried times that jumped back and forth and the player showed them out of step. The hardware path now gives each picture the earliest time among the packets fed and not yet matched, which is the display-order time, and also stops negative times wrapping to 4295 s.

- [x] Keep buffering while paused, so a video can be played on without a network. Tried on 2026-10-07 as a 512 MiB window that kept filling itself as blocks arrived: it saturated the Wi-Fi link for minutes after every open, so the block the decoder needed queued behind 31 prefetch reads, playback stuttered and seeks crawled. Rolled back the same night. Performance of first load, seeking and playback comes first; any new attempt must throttle prefetch well below the link's spare capacity and give the block needed now priority. Done 2026-10-11 the other way round: nothing is fetched ahead while playing. A small button under play (an arrow into a tray) pauses the video and fills a reserve past the read-ahead window, up to 1 GiB, with 8 reads in flight; play (or the button again) stops it, and whatever landed stays. The seek bar shows the stretch held and the label beside the button counts it ("Buffering… 412 MB of 1 GB", "1 GB buffered"). The reader takes blocks from the reserve as it reaches them, so the rest plays with the network gone; what it holds behind the playhead is dropped only as room is needed, so a seek back within it works too.

- [x] Seeking froze the picture for seconds, backwards worst of all. The decoder decoded and dropped everything from the keyframe up to the target; it now shows the keyframe at once and plays on from there (a jump forward never lands behind where it started). After a jump the reader also requests the first four blocks straight away instead of waiting for the old window's reads to drain, since a keyframe spans several. Backwards was still 5–10 s on the Frame (2026-10-10): FFmpeg's V4L2 decoder has no flush, so the pictures queued before a seek came out first, the player took the first of them as the landing point and decoded-and-dropped everything from the keyframe back up to the old position. The decoder now counts the pictures it still holds at a seek and drops them; a seek lands in 40–130 ms either way. The read-ahead window also restarts at four blocks after a jump and grows with sequential reading, so a seek soon after another no longer waits behind 32 MiB of the old position's reads.
